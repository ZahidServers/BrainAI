SUBJECT: Language
CHAPTER: Letters and Sounds
============================================================

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
Recognise familiar letters, connect sounds with letters, listen and respond to simple words.

TEACHING TEXT
Letters are symbols we use to write language. A letter can have a name and a sound. Children can practise one sound at a time by listening, repeating, identifying the beginning sound of a word, and matching a picture to its first letter.

KEY TERMS
letter, sound, word, picture, vowel

EXAMPLES / APPLICATIONS
The word 'sun' begins with the /s/ sound. The word 'ball' begins with /b/.

PRACTICE QUESTIONS
1. Name a letter you know.
2. Which sound begins 'cat'?
3. Match a picture of a ball with its beginning sound.

ANSWER KEY
1. Answers vary.
2. /k/.
3. B/b.

MISCONCEPTION CHECK
Students should distinguish the formal concept from everyday
language and should explain their reasoning rather than memorising
only the final answer.

REVISION

questions before progressing to the next chapter.

SUBJECT: Mathematics
CHAPTER: Counting and Comparing
============================================================

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
Count objects reliably, recognise small quantities, and compare groups.

TEACHING TEXT
Counting means saying number names in a fixed order while matching one number name to one object. The final number said tells how many objects are in the group. Groups can be compared as more, fewer, or the same.

KEY TERMS
count, number, more, fewer, same

EXAMPLES / APPLICATIONS
Three apples form a group of three. A group of five has more apples than a group of three.

PRACTICE QUESTIONS
1. Count four stars.
2. Which is more: 2 blocks or 5 blocks?
3. What does 'same' mean?

ANSWER KEY
1. Four.
2. Five blocks.
3. Equal in number.

MISCONCEPTION CHECK
Students should distinguish the formal concept from everyday
language and should explain their reasoning rather than memorising
only the final answer.

Words combine to express ideas. A sentence is a group of words that communicates a complete thought. Stories usually have characters, a setting, events, and an ending. Children can retell a story in the correct order.

CHAPTER: Numbers to 100
============================================================

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
Read, write, count and compare numbers and use simple addition and subtraction.

TEACHING TEXT
Numbers describe quantity and position. The place of a digit affects its value. In a two-digit number, the first digit counts tens and the second counts ones. Addition combines quantities; subtraction finds what remains or the difference.

KEY TERMS
ones, tens, addition, subtraction, greater, smaller

EXAMPLES / APPLICATIONS
34 means 3 tens and 4 ones. 7 + 2 means combine seven objects with two more to get nine.

PRACTICE QUESTIONS

2. Calculate 7 + 2.
3. Calculate 10 - 3.

ANSWER KEY
1. Four tens.
2. 9.
3. 7.

language and should explain their reasoning rather than memorising
