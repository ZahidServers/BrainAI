CHAPTER G6M-A — Integers ("The Other Side of Zero") — FULL APPARATUS
METADATA. Grade 6 · NCERT · Maths · Unit: Number system · Prereq: whole numbers, number line (G5) · Difficulty: Medium · Objectives: understand negative numbers and the extended number line; represent integers; compare/order; add, subtract, multiply and divide integers using sign rules; apply to real contexts (temperature, debt, elevation).

TEACHING MATERIAL.
Why negatives? Whole numbers can't describe "3 below zero" or "owing ₹50." Integers extend numbers to the left of zero: …−3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3… Positive integers are to the right of 0, negative to the left; zero is neither positive nor negative.
The number line. Numbers increase left→right, so −5 < −2 < 0 < 3. Two numbers the same distance from 0 on opposite sides are opposites (+4 and −4). The absolute value is distance from 0 (  |  −4  |  = 4).
Adding integers.
 • Same sign → add the values, keep the sign: (−3)+(−5) = −8; (+3)+(+5)=+8.
 • Different signs → subtract the smaller value from the larger, take the sign of the larger-value number: (−7)+(+3) = −4; (+7)+(−3)=+4.
Subtracting integers. Subtracting is adding the opposite: a − b = a + (−b). So 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8; (−4) − (+6) = −4 + (−6) = −10.
Multiplying/dividing integers (sign rules).
 • (+)(+) = + ; (−)(−) = + ; (+)(−) = − ; (−)(+) = − . Same signs → positive; different signs → negative.
 • Examples: (−4)×(−3)=+12; (−4)×(+3)=−12; (−12)÷(+4)=−3; (−12)÷(−4)=+3.
Properties. Integers are closed under +, −, × (result is always an integer). Addition/multiplication are commutative and associative; 0 is the additive identity, 1 the multiplicative identity; every integer has an opposite (additive inverse).

Worked examples.
1. Order −3, 5, −7, 0, 2 → −7, −3, 0, 2, 5. 2. (−8)+(+5) = −3. 3. 6 − (−4) = 10. 4. (−5)×(−6) = 30. 5. (−48)÷(6) = −8. 6. Temperature rises from −4°C by 9°C → −4+9 = 5°C.

Visual/diagram description. A horizontal number line from −7 to +7 with 0 centred; arrows show (−3)+(+5): start at −3, move 5 right, land on +2.

DEFINITIONS (10). integer, positive integer, negative integer, number line, opposite (additive inverse), absolute value, closure, additive identity, sign rule, comparison (< >).

Q&A. Easy: Which is greater, −2 or −5? (−2.)  |  −9  |  ? (9.) Medium: (−6)+(−4)? (−10.) 3−(−7)? (10.) Difficult: (−7)×(+8)? (−56.) (−72)÷(−9)? (8.) Application: A diver at −20 m rises 12 m — new depth? (−8 m.) A bank balance of −₹300 gets a ₹500 deposit — balance? (+₹200.) Reasoning: Why is (−)×(−) positive? (Removing a negative amount repeatedly increases the total; consistent with distributivity: (−a)(−b)+(−a)(b)=(−a)(−b+b)=0, so (−a)(−b) must equal +ab.)

CHAPTER G7M-A — Algebraic Expressions & Simple Equations — FULL APPARATUS
METADATA. Grade 7 · NCERT · Maths · Prereq: integers, patterns · Medium · Objectives: use variables/letters for unknowns; form and read algebraic expressions; identify terms, coefficients, like/unlike terms; add/subtract expressions; understand an equation and solve simple linear equations in one variable; translate word problems into equations.

TEACHING MATERIAL.
From arithmetic to algebra. A variable is a letter (x, y, n) standing for an unknown or changing number. "5 more than a number" = x + 5; "twice a number" = 2x; "a number divided by 3" = x/3.
Expressions. An algebraic expression combines variables, numbers and operations (e.g., 3x + 7). A term is a part separated by + or − (in 3x + 7, the terms are 3x and 7). In the term 3x, 3 is the coefficient and x is the variable. Constants are pure numbers (7). Like terms have the same variable part (3x and 5x); unlike terms don't (3x and 5y).
Operations on expressions. Combine like terms: 3x + 5x = 8x; 7y − 2y = 5y; 4x + 3 + 2x = 6x + 3. You cannot combine unlike terms (3x + 2y stays as is).
Equations. An equation says two expressions are equal (has an = sign), e.g., 2x + 3 = 11. Solving means finding the variable value that makes it true. Use the balance idea: do the same operation to both sides.
 • 2x + 3 = 11 → subtract 3: 2x = 8 → divide by 2: x = 4. Check: 2(4)+3 = 11 ✓.
 • x/5 = 3 → multiply by 5: x = 15.
Word problems. "Three times a number, increased by 4, is 19." → 3x + 4 = 19 → 3x = 15 → x = 5.

Worked examples.
1. Simplify 6a + 2 − 4a + 5 = 2a + 7. 2. Solve 5x − 7 = 18 → 5x = 25 → x = 5. 3. Solve (x/3) + 2 = 6 → x/3 = 4 → x = 12. 4. Perimeter of a square is 4s; if perimeter = 36, s = 9.

Visual/diagram description. A two-pan balance: left pan "2x + 3", right pan "11"; removing 3 unit-weights from both pans keeps balance (2x = 8); splitting into 2 equal groups gives x = 4.

DEFINITIONS (10). variable, constant, algebraic expression, term, coefficient, like terms, unlike terms, equation, solution/root, linear equation.

Q&A. Easy: Coefficient of x in 7x? (7.) Are 3x and 5x like terms? (Yes.) Medium: Simplify 4y + 3 − y (3y + 3). Solve x + 9 = 15 (x = 6). Difficult: Solve 3x − 5 = 2x + 4 (x = 9). Simplify 5a − 2b + 3a + b (8a − b). Application: A pencil costs ₹x; 4 pencils cost ₹28 — find x. (4x = 28 → x = 7.) Reasoning: Why can't 3x + 2y be simplified to 5xy? (Unlike terms; adding counts of different things, and x·y is multiplication, not addition.)

MCQs (12, abridged). Coefficient of y in −6y: −6; like terms: 2a & 5a; simplify 3x+4x: 7x; solve x−4=10: 14; solve 2x=18: 9; a variable is a: letter for a number; terms in 5x+3: 2; solve x/2=6: 12; 3x+2 at x=4: 14; equation has: = sign; solve 4x+1=13: 3; simplify 7m−7m: 0.

T/F (10). 3x and 3y are like terms F. An equation has an = sign T. 5x + 2x = 7x T. The coefficient of x in x is 0 F (1). Solving keeps both sides equal T. 3x + 4 is an equation F (expression). x = 4 solves 2x+3=11 T. Constants have no variable T. 2y − 2y = 2 F (0). You may add the same number to both sides of an equation T.

Fact sheet: like terms combine; a·x notation; solve linear ax + b = c → x = (c − b)/a.

CHAPTER G8S-A — Photosynthesis / Nutrition in Plants — FULL APPARATUS
METADATA. Grade 8 (also revisits Grade 7 biology) · NCERT · Science · Prereq: plant parts, cell basics · Medium · Objectives: define nutrition and its modes; explain photosynthesis (raw materials, conditions, products, site); describe the role of chlorophyll, stomata and the leaf; distinguish autotrophs/heterotrophs; explain other modes (parasitic, saprotrophic, insectivorous, symbiotic); connect to food chains.

TEACHING MATERIAL.
Nutrition. All living things need food for energy, growth and repair. Autotrophs (green plants) make their own food; heterotrophs (animals, fungi) depend on others.
Photosynthesis. The process by which green plants make food (glucose) using carbon dioxide + water, in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll, releasing oxygen.
 Word equation: carbon dioxide + water —(sunlight, chlorophyll)→ glucose + oxygen.
 Balanced (for reference): 6CO₂ + 6H₂O —light→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.
Where & how. Mostly in leaves (the "food factories"). Chlorophyll (green pigment in chloroplasts) captures light energy. Stomata (tiny leaf pores) let CO₂ in and O₂ out; roots absorb water, carried up by xylem. Glucose made is used for energy or stored as starch (tested with iodine → blue-black).
Two steps (idea). Light reactions capture energy; that energy is used to combine CO₂ and water into glucose.
Other nutrition modes. Parasitic (Cuscuta/amarbel takes food from a host), saprotrophic (fungi digest dead matter externally), insectivorous (pitcher plant traps insects for nitrogen), symbiotic (lichen = alga + fungus; Rhizobium in legume roots fixes nitrogen).
Why it matters. Photosynthesis is the source of almost all food and oxygen on Earth; it begins every food chain and removes CO₂.

Experiment/activity. Starch test: de-starch a plant (dark 2–3 days), expose one leaf to light with part covered by black paper, then test with iodine — only the light-exposed part turns blue-black, showing light and chlorophyll are needed. Safety: handle boiling water/alcohol away from flame (alcohol is flammable — heat in a water bath).

Visual/diagram description. A leaf cross-section: upper/lower epidermis, palisade cells packed with green chloroplasts, air spaces, and a stoma with two guard cells; arrows show CO₂ in, O₂ out, water up from the vein.

DEFINITIONS (10). nutrition, autotroph, heterotroph, photosynthesis, chlorophyll, chloroplast, stomata, xylem, starch, saprotroph.

Q&A. Easy: Which gas do plants take in for photosynthesis? (Carbon dioxide.) What is released? (Oxygen.) Medium: Name the green pigment and where it sits. (Chlorophyll, in chloroplasts.) What are stomata for? (Gas exchange.) Difficult: Write the word equation of photosynthesis. State two conditions necessary. (Sunlight, chlorophyll.) Application: Why do farmers avoid overcrowding crops? (Enough light for photosynthesis.) A variegated leaf (green + white) tested with iodine — which part turns blue-black? (Only the green part — it has chlorophyll.) Reasoning: Why is photosynthesis called the basis of life? (It provides food and oxygen and starts food chains.)

CHAPTER G8SS-A — Civics: The Constitution & Democracy — MEDIUM ANCHOR
METADATA. Grade 8 · NCERT · Social Science (Civics) · Prereq: idea of rules/government (G6–7) · Medium · Objectives: explain why a country needs a constitution; key features of the Indian Constitution; fundamental rights & duties; the three organs of government; the meaning of democracy and secularism.
TEACHING. A constitution is the supreme rule-book that lays out how a country is governed, the powers of government, and citizens' rights — protecting people from misuse of power. India's Constitution (adopted 26 Nov 1949, in force 26 Jan 1950) makes India a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic. Fundamental Rights (equality, freedom, against exploitation, freedom of religion, cultural/educational, constitutional remedies) protect citizens; Fundamental Duties list responsibilities. Government has three organs — Legislature (makes laws), Executive (implements), Judiciary (interprets and protects rights) — with separation of powers. Democracy = rule by the people through elected representatives; secularism = the state treats all religions equally.
FACT vs INTERPRETATION vs OPINION. FACT: the Constitution came into force on 26 Jan 1950. INTERPRETATION: historians argue the Constitution balanced diverse interests through compromise. OPINION: "It is the world's best constitution" is a value judgement, not a fact.
DEFINITIONS (10). constitution, sovereign, secular, democracy, republic, fundamental rights, fundamental duties, legislature, executive, judiciary.
Q&A. Easy: When did the Constitution come into force? (26 Jan 1950.) Medium: Name the three organs of government. (Legislature, executive, judiciary.) Application: Which right lets you approach courts if a right is violated? (Right to constitutional remedies.) Reasoning: Why separate the three organs? (To prevent concentration/misuse of power — checks and balances.)
MCQs (6, abridged). Supreme law: Constitution; makes laws: legislature; interprets laws: judiciary; equal treatment of religions: secularism; rule by people: democracy; rights that protect citizens: fundamental rights.
HOTS. How do fundamental rights and the judiciary work together to protect a citizen? (Courts can strike down laws/actions violating rights.) Why might duties matter as much as rights? (Rights of some depend on duties of others.)
MISCONCEPTIONS. "The government is above the Constitution" → the Constitution is supreme; government works within it. "Secular means anti-religion" → it means equal treatment of all religions.
REVISION. Constitution = supreme rule-book; India = sovereign socialist secular democratic republic; rights & duties; three organs & separation of powers; democracy & secularism.
ADAPTIVE — "constitution". L1: "A constitution is a country's main rule-book." L2: "It sets how the government works, what powers it has, and what rights citizens are guaranteed — so no one can misuse power." L3: "A constitution is the foundational legal document establishing the structure, powers, and limits of state institutions and enumerating justiciable rights." L4: "Like the rules of a game everyone — even the referee — must follow, so play stays fair." L5: "When a court cancels an unfair law, it's using the Constitution to protect ordinary citizens' freedoms."
