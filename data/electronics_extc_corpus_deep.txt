CURRICULUM BASIS
----------------
This corpus is based on the user's supplied roadmap spanning Grades 9–12,
B.Sc./M.Sc. Electronics, B.E./B.Tech. Electronics, Electronics & Telecommunication
Engineering (EXTC), and M.E./M.Tech. VLSI, Embedded, Communication and Signal
Processing.

COVERAGE
--------
Foundations: charge, current, voltage, resistance, power, capacitors, inductors,
series/parallel circuits, KCL, KVL, magnetism, induction, AC/DC.

Semiconductors: band theory, intrinsic/extrinsic materials, carrier transport,
PN junctions, Shockley equation, breakdown, rectifiers, BJT, FET, MOSFET,
optoelectronics, LEDs, photodiodes and solar cells.

Analog: transistor biasing, small-signal models, amplifiers, feedback, oscillators,
op-amps, instrumentation, active/passive filters and frequency response.

Digital: number systems, gates, Boolean algebra, K-maps, flip-flops, counters,
registers, memories, timing, hazards, HDL, FSMs, ADC/DAC.

Embedded: 8085, 8051, ARM Cortex-M, interrupts, timers, GPIO, I2C, SPI, CAN,
RTOS, scheduling and synchronization.

Signals/DSP: continuous/discrete signals, LTI systems, convolution, Fourier,
Laplace, Z-transform, sampling, aliasing, DFT, FFT, FIR, IIR and adaptive filters.

Communication/EXTC: AM/FM/PM, PCM, ASK/FSK/PSK/QPSK, information theory, source
coding, error-control coding, eye diagrams, detection, fading, MIMO, OFDM.

RF/Microwave: transmission lines, reflection coefficient, VSWR, Smith chart,
antennas, waveguides, S-parameters, radar and propagation.

Optical: fiber, numerical aperture, attenuation, dispersion, EDFA, WDM and PON.

Systems: cellular networks, IoT protocols, control systems, power electronics,
CMOS VLSI, short-channel effects, low-power design, computer architecture,
verification/testing, SoC interconnects and physical design.

DISCIPLINE : Foundations
CHAPTER    : Electricity Fundamentals
TOPIC      : Electric charge and current
DIFFICULTY : Easy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DEFINITION
Electric charge is a physical quantity associated with electric interactions; current is the rate at which charge crosses a reference surface.

2. DEEP THEORY / EXPLANATION
Conventional current is defined from positive to negative potential in circuit diagrams even though electron drift in metallic conductors is opposite. Current is a flow variable, while charge is the accumulated quantity.

3. CORE CONCEPTS
1. charge
2. current
3. conventional current
4. electron drift
5. current direction

4. KEY EQUATIONS / RELATIONSHIPS
1. I=dQ/dt
2. Q=It for constant current

5. APPLICATIONS
1. battery circuits
2. LED circuits
3. sensors
4. power distribution

6. COMPARISONS
1. current vs charge

7. LABORATORY / MEASUREMENT EXAMPLES
