BRAINAI LANGUAGE CORPUS — HINDI
KINDERGARTEN TO GRADE 12
COMPREHENSIVE GRAMMAR, LANGUAGE SKILLS, LITERATURE AND WRITING

Purpose:
This corpus is designed as structured educational knowledge for BrainAI tutoring,
question generation, explanation, revision, assessment and language-learning systems.

Scope:
KG through Grade 12, with progressive difficulty. It covers the developmental
framework supplied by the user plus a universal language-learning framework.
Exact textbook chapters differ by board, state, school and academic year; therefore
this is a master pedagogical corpus rather than a reproduction of any one textbook.

SCRIPT:
देवनागरी

LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC ARCHITECTURE:
हिंदी is predominantly SOV; postpositions follow noun phrases; gender and number affect agreement; verb morphology encodes tense/aspect/person and participial constructions.


UNIVERSAL LANGUAGE SKILLS
1. Listening: sound discrimination, comprehension, note-taking, inference, response.
2. Speaking: pronunciation, fluency, vocabulary, dialogue, presentation, debate.
3. Reading: decoding, fluency, literal comprehension, inference, critical reading.
4. Writing: handwriting/script, spelling, sentence construction, paragraphs, essays, formal writing.
5. Vocabulary: synonyms, antonyms, homonyms/homophones where applicable, collocations, idioms, proverbs, word families.
6. Grammar: morphology, syntax, agreement, tense/aspect/mood, case/postpositions, sentence types.
7. Literature: prose, poetry, drama, narrative, character, theme, imagery, symbolism, tone, authorial intent.
8. Composition: descriptive, narrative, expository, argumentative, reflective and functional writing.
9. Editing: spelling, punctuation, agreement, word choice, sentence order, redundancy, ambiguity and style.
10. Assessment: recall, application, transformation, error correction, unseen comprehension, writing and oral performance.

BRAINAI RESPONSE AND TEACHING PROTOCOL
================================================================================
When teaching a concept:
1. Give the learner's level-appropriate definition.
2. Give the form/rule.
3. Explain the function.
4. Show 3–10 examples.
5. Show a common incorrect form.
6. Explain why it is incorrect.
7. Give controlled exercises.
8. Give production exercises.
9. Test comprehension with an unseen example.
10. Increase difficulty only after mastery.

For bilingual learners:
- Give the target-language explanation first.
- Use the learner's known language only as support.
- Do not assume literal translation preserves grammar.
- Explicitly identify differences in word order, gender, case, tense/aspect,
  agreement, script and idiomatic usage.

For text correction:
- Preserve intended meaning.
- Correct spelling, grammar, punctuation and word choice.
- Explain the rule responsible for each correction.
- Distinguish grammatical error from stylistic preference.

For literature:
- Separate literal meaning, vocabulary, grammar, theme, structure, imagery,
  rhetoric, cultural context and critical interpretation.
- Never invent a quotation and attribute it to a real author.

For assessment:
Generate MCQs, fill-in-the-blanks, matching, short answers, transformations,
error correction, translation, unseen passages, dictation, speaking prompts,
composition prompts, viva questions and model answers.

For language generation:
- Maintain correct script direction.
- Preserve diacritics when the task requires them.
- Avoid mixing scripts unless requested.
- Respect regional/dialectal variation, especially for Konkani.
