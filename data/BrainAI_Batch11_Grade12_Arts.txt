★ CHAPTER G12PSY-INT — Intelligence & Psychological Attributes — FULLER ANCHOR
1. METADATA
• Grade 12 · NCERT · Humanities · Psychology · Unit: Variations in psychological attributes · Prereq: Grade 11 methods & concepts · Difficulty: Medium · Objectives: define intelligence & individual differences; compare major theories; understand IQ and its assessment; discuss culture, and concepts like aptitude, creativity, and emotional intelligence.
2. TEACHING MATERIAL
(a) Individual differences & intelligence. People differ in psychological attributes; intelligence is the ability to understand, learn from experience, reason, and adapt to the environment. Definitions vary by theory.
(b) Theories of intelligence.
 • Psychometric / Spearman's two-factor: a general factor ‘g’ underlies all intelligent behaviour, plus specific factors ‘s’.
 • Thurstone's Primary Mental Abilities: intelligence = several independent abilities (verbal, numerical, spatial, reasoning, etc.).
 • Gardner's Multiple Intelligences: eight relatively independent intelligences — linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily-kinaesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalistic.
 • Sternberg's Triarchic theory: componential (analytical), experiential (creative), contextual (practical) intelligence.
 • PASS model (Das): intelligence as Planning, Attention-arousal, and Simultaneous-Successive processing.
(c) IQ & assessment. IQ = (Mental Age / Chronological Age) × 100 (original ratio IQ). IQ scores form a roughly normal distribution (mean ≈ 100). Assessment uses verbal, non-verbal and performance tests, individual or group, and must be reliable, valid and culturally fair. Categories range from intellectual disability to giftedness.
(d) Culture & intelligence. Western views stress cognitive/analytical skills; Indian (e.g., buddhi) and other traditions include social, emotional and practical competence — intelligence is partly culturally defined.
(e) Related concepts. Aptitude (potential to acquire a skill), creativity (novel & useful ideas; not identical to IQ), emotional intelligence (perceiving, understanding and regulating emotions — EQ).

FACT/INTERPRETATION/OPINION. FACT: IQ tests yield scores distributed around 100. INTERPRETATION: Gardner argues intelligence is plural, not a single g. OPINION: "IQ tests are useless" — a value/empirical claim requiring evidence and nuance.

A. DEFINITIONS (10)
intelligence, ‘g’ factor, multiple intelligences, triarchic theory, IQ, mental age, aptitude, creativity, emotional intelligence, reliability.

B. QUESTIONS & ANSWERS
Easy: What does IQ stand for? (Intelligence Quotient.) Who proposed ‘g’? (Spearman.) Medium: Name Sternberg's three intelligences. (Analytical, creative, practical.) Difficult: How is ratio IQ computed? ((MA/CA)×100.) Application: A child, MA 10, CA 8 — IQ? ((10/8)×100 = 125.) Reasoning: Why must a good test be culturally fair? (To avoid bias against groups unfamiliar with the content.)

C. MCQs (12, abridged)
IQ = (MA/CA)×100; ‘g’ factor → Spearman; eight intelligences → Gardner; analytical/creative/practical → Sternberg; average IQ ≈ 100; potential to learn a skill = aptitude; novel & useful thinking = creativity; EQ concerns emotions; a consistent test is reliable; a test that measures what it claims is valid; intelligence distribution is roughly normal; PASS model by Das.

D. TRUE/FALSE (6)
Spearman proposed multiple intelligences. F (Gardner). IQ = (MA/CA)×100. T. Creativity always equals high IQ. F. EQ involves managing emotions. T. A valid test is one that gives consistent scores. F (that's reliability). Intelligence is defined identically across cultures. F.

E. FILL BLANKS (6)

F/G. SHORT/LONG
Short: Compute IQ for MA 12, CA 10. (120.) Long (model): "Compare the single-factor and multiple-intelligence views of intelligence." Model — Spearman's psychometric view holds a general factor ‘g’ underlies all intelligent performance, so one summary score is meaningful. Gardner challenges this, arguing for several relatively independent intelligences (linguistic, spatial, musical, interpersonal, etc.), so a single IQ misses a person's diverse strengths. This is an ongoing theoretical debate (interpretation), not a settled fact; each view has evidence and critics.

H. HOTS
Why might a highly creative person score only average on a standard IQ test? (IQ tests emphasise convergent, not divergent, thinking.) Can two people with the same IQ differ greatly in real-life success? (Yes — practical & emotional intelligence, motivation, opportunity matter.)

I. MISCONCEPTIONS
"IQ fully determines success" → many factors matter. "Intelligence is fixed and single" → theories dispute this; it's multidimensional and develops. "Creativity = intelligence" → related but distinct.

J. REVISION
Definitions & individual differences; theories (Spearman g, Thurstone PMA, Gardner MI, Sternberg triarchic, PASS); IQ & normal distribution; test qualities (reliable/valid/fair); culture; aptitude, creativity, EQ.

ADAPTIVE — "multiple intelligences"
L1: "There are many kinds of being smart, not just one." L2: "Gardner says people can be intelligent in different ways — with words, numbers, music, movement, understanding others, and more — so a single IQ number is too narrow." L3: "Gardner's theory posits several relatively autonomous intelligences with distinct neural and developmental profiles, challenging a unitary g." L4: "Intelligence is like a dashboard with many gauges — verbal, spatial, musical, social — not a single fuel meter." L5: "A gifted dancer, a math whiz, and an empathetic counsellor are each 'intelligent' in a different domain — that's multiple intelligences in real life."

★ CHAPTER G12PS-COLDWAR — The Cold War & the Changing World Order — FULLER ANCHOR
METADATA. Grade 12 · NCERT · Political Science · Unit: Contemporary World Politics · Prereq: WWII basics · Difficulty: Medium · Objectives: explain the Cold War & bipolarity; non-alignment; the end of bipolarity; US hegemony and emerging multipolarity — with FACT/INTERPRETATION/OPINION.
TEACHING.
• The Cold War (1945–1991). After WWII, two superpowers — the USA (capitalist bloc, NATO) and the USSR (communist bloc, Warsaw Pact) — competed globally without direct war (hence "cold"), through arms races, ideology, alliances and proxy conflicts (Korea, Vietnam, Cuba). Deterrence and the fear of mutual destruction (nuclear weapons) restrained direct war.
• Bipolarity. The world was largely organised around these two poles; smaller states often aligned with one.
• Non-Aligned Movement (NAM). Many newly independent nations (India, Egypt, Yugoslavia, Indonesia, Ghana — Nehru, Nasser, Tito) chose not to join either bloc, keeping independent foreign policy and promoting decolonisation and development. NAM was not neutrality/passivity but active engagement on their own terms.
• End of the Cold War. The USSR's economic weaknesses, reforms (glasnost, perestroika), and popular movements led to the collapse of the Soviet Union (1991) and the end of bipolarity.
• After bipolarity. The US emerged as the sole superpower (hegemony); globalisation deepened; new centres (EU, China, India, Russia) point toward multipolarity. Debates continue on US dominance, its limits, and a shifting order.
FACT/INTERPRETATION/OPINION. FACT: The USSR dissolved in 1991. INTERPRETATION: Historians debate the relative weight of economic vs ideological causes. OPINION: "A unipolar world is best for peace" — a contested value judgement.
DEFINITIONS (8). Cold War, bipolarity, superpower, deterrence, Non-Aligned Movement, hegemony, multipolarity, proxy war.
Q&A. Easy: Which two superpowers led the Cold War? (USA & USSR.) When did the USSR collapse? (1991.) Medium: What was NAM's stance? (Non-alignment with either bloc, independent policy.) Application: Why were conflicts like Vietnam called "proxy wars"? (Superpowers backed opposing sides without fighting each other directly.) Reasoning: Why did nuclear weapons restrain direct war? (Mutual assured destruction — deterrence.)
MCQs (8, abridged). Cold War poles: USA & USSR; military bloc of the West: NATO; India's stance: non-aligned; USSR collapse year: 1991; single dominant power: hegemony/unipolarity; fear-based restraint: deterrence; NAM leaders included Nehru, Nasser and Tito; conflicts fought via allies: proxy wars.
HOTS. Was non-alignment a sign of weakness or strategy? (A reasoned strategy for autonomy — present both views.) Is today's world unipolar or multipolar? (Contested — evidence for both; an interpretive debate.)
MISCONCEPTIONS. "Cold War had no fighting" → there were proxy wars, just not direct superpower war. "Non-alignment meant staying neutral/inactive" → it was active independent engagement.
REVISION. Cold War & bipolarity; NATO/Warsaw Pact; deterrence; NAM; 1991 collapse; US hegemony → emerging multipolarity; FACT/INTERPRETATION/OPINION.
ADAPTIVE — "non-alignment". L1: "Non-alignment meant not joining either superpower's side." L2: "Newly free nations like India refused to permanently join the US or Soviet bloc, keeping the freedom to decide each issue on its merits." L3: "Non-alignment was a foreign-policy doctrine asserting strategic autonomy and active agency for post-colonial states within a bipolar order." L4: "It's like refusing to permanently join either of two rival gangs at school — staying friendly-but-independent so you keep your own voice." L5: "India's ability to trade and talk with both blocs during the Cold War, without being anyone's satellite, is non-alignment in practice."

★ CHAPTER G12SO-INDIA — Indian Society & Social Change — FULLER ANCHOR
METADATA. Grade 12 · NCERT · Sociology · Unit: Indian Society / Social Change & Development · Prereq: Grade 11 foundations · Difficulty: Medium · Objectives: understand India's demographic structure; caste, tribe, class, family; social change processes; the impact of colonialism, industrialisation, urbanisation, globalisation; movements.
TEACHING.
• Demographic structure. Population size, growth, sex ratio, age structure; the demographic dividend (a large working-age population) and its challenges. Concepts: birth/death rate, fertility, migration.
• Social institutions in India. Caste (a system of hereditary, ranked groups; changing but persistent), tribe (distinct communities, often outside caste), family & kinship (joint vs nuclear; regional diversity), class (economic strata), gender (patriarchy and change).
• Structural change & colonialism. British rule reshaped the economy (deindustrialisation, land systems), introduced modern institutions, and created new classes.
• Processes of change. Sanskritisation (lower groups adopting higher-caste practices to raise status), Westernisation (adopting Western lifestyles/values), modernisation, secularisation, urbanisation and industrialisation — each with mixed effects.
• Development & its costs. Planned development, the Green Revolution (higher output, but regional/ecological concerns), industrialisation, displacement, and environmental impact.
• Social movements. Peasant, workers', women's, environmental, and caste/tribal movements as agents of change.
FACT/INTERPRETATION/OPINION. FACT: India's 2011 Census recorded specific population figures. INTERPRETATION: Sociologists debate whether caste is weakening or merely changing form. OPINION: "Urbanisation is destroying Indian culture" — a value judgement.
DEFINITIONS (10). demography, sex ratio, demographic dividend, caste, tribe, class, Sanskritisation, Westernisation, urbanisation, social movement.
Q&A. Easy: What is the sex ratio? (Females per 1000 males.) Define a social movement. (Organised collective effort for/against change.) Medium: What is Sanskritisation? Difficult: Distinguish Westernisation and modernisation. Application: How can a "demographic dividend" become a burden? (If jobs/education don't match the youth bulge.) Reasoning: Why say caste is "changing, not vanishing"? (Its ritual form weakens, but it persists in politics, marriage, inequality.)
MCQs (10, abridged). Females per 1000 males: sex ratio; large working-age population: demographic dividend; hereditary ranked groups: caste; adopting higher-caste practices: Sanskritisation; adopting Western ways: Westernisation; shift to city living: urbanisation; study of population: demography; Green Revolution raised agricultural output; organised push for change: social movement; joint vs nuclear concerns the family.
T/F (5). Sanskritisation is adopting Western dress. F. Sex ratio = females per 1000 males. T. A demographic dividend is automatically beneficial. F (needs jobs/education). Caste has completely disappeared. F. Social movements can drive change. T.
FILL BLANKS (5). Females per 1000 males is the  (sex ratio). Lower groups emulating higher castes =  (Sanskritisation). A youthful working-age bulge is the demographic  (dividend). Movement to cities is  (urbanisation). Organised collective action for change is a  (social movement).
HOTS. How can development simultaneously reduce poverty and cause displacement? (Dams/industry raise output but uproot communities — a trade-off.) Why might the same reform (Green Revolution) be judged success and failure? (Higher yields vs inequality/ecological costs — interpretive.)
MISCONCEPTIONS. "Caste = only religion" → it's a social-structural system affecting economy/politics. "Modernisation = Westernisation" → related but distinct. "Demographic dividend guarantees growth" → only if productively employed.
REVISION. Demography & dividend; caste/tribe/class/family/gender; colonial structural change; Sanskritisation/Westernisation/modernisation/urbanisation; development costs; social movements.

Srinivas: a caste or group adopts the rituals, diet and lifestyle of a higher caste to claim higher social status over time." L4: "It's like adopting the manners of a more prestigious group to be accepted among them — climbing the ladder by imitation."
