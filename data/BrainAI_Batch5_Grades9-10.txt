===== GRADE 10 (NCERT) — MATHEMATICS =====

★ FLAGSHIP CHAPTER G10M-QUAD — Quadratic Equations — FULL APPARATUS
1. METADATA
• Grade 10 · Board NCERT/CBSE · Stream — · Subject Mathematics · Unit: Algebra · Chapter: Quadratic Equations
• Prerequisites: what an equation is; polynomials and degree; linear equations; factorisation; square roots; the number line and integers.
• Difficulty: Medium–Hard (board weightage high; algebra unit ~20 marks).
• Learning objectives: recognise a quadratic equation and its standard form; identify coefficients and roots; solve by factorisation, completing the square, and the quadratic formula; use the discriminant to judge the nature of roots; relate roots to coefficients; form and solve word problems; interpret roots graphically.
2. TEACHING MATERIAL (built up from first principles)

(a) What is an equation? An equation states that two expressions are equal, joined by "=". Solving means finding the value(s) of the unknown that make the statement true.

(b) What does "degree" mean? The degree of a polynomial is the highest power of the variable. A linear equation has degree 1 (ax + b = 0). A quadratic equation has degree 2 — the variable's highest power is 2.

(c) What makes an equation quadratic? It must contain an x² term (with a non-zero coefficient) and no higher power. The standard form is:
  ax² + bx + c = 0, where a, b, c are real numbers and a ≠ 0.
 • a = coefficient of x² (leading coefficient); b = coefficient of x; c = constant term. If a = 0 it collapses to a linear equation, so a ≠ 0 is essential.
 Examples: 2x² − 5x + 3 = 0 (a=2, b=−5, c=3); x² − 9 = 0 (a=1, b=0, c=−9); x² + x = 0 (c=0).

(d) Roots (solutions). A root is a value of x that satisfies the equation (makes LHS = 0). A quadratic has at most two roots. If α is a root, then aα² + bα + c = 0.

(e) Graphical meaning. The graph of y = ax² + bx + c is a parabola (U-shaped if a>0, ∩-shaped if a<0). The roots are the x-values where the parabola crosses the x-axis (y = 0). Three cases: it may cut the axis at two points (two distinct real roots), touch it at one point (two equal roots), or not meet it (no real roots).

(f) Method 1 — Factorisation (splitting the middle term). Write ax² + bx + c as a product of two linear factors, then set each to zero (zero-product rule: if PQ = 0 then P = 0 or Q = 0).
 Procedure: find two numbers whose product = a·c and sum = b; split bx accordingly; group and factor.
 Example: 2x² − 5x + 3 = 0. a·c = 6; need factors of 6 summing to −5 → −2 and −3. So 2x² − 2x − 3x + 3 = 0 → 2x(x−1) − 3(x−1) = 0 → (x−1)(2x−3) = 0 → x = 1 or x = 3/2.

(g) Method 2 — Completing the square. Make a perfect-square trinomial.
 Procedure: divide by a; move the constant; add (half the x-coefficient)² to both sides; write as (x + p)²; take square roots.
 Example: x² + 6x + 5 = 0 → x² + 6x = −5 → add 9 → x² + 6x + 9 = 4 → (x+3)² = 4 → x + 3 = ±2 → x = −1 or x = −5.
 This method is also how the general quadratic formula is derived.

(h) Method 3 — Quadratic formula (works for every quadratic).
  x = [ −b ± √(b² − 4ac) ] / (2a)
 Derivation (sketch): from ax² + bx + c = 0, divide by a, complete the square to (x + b/2a)² = (b² − 4ac)/4a², take roots, and rearrange.
 Example: 3x² − 2x − 1 = 0 → b²−4ac = 4+12 = 16 → x = (2 ± 4)/6 → x = 1 or x = −1/3.

(i) Discriminant D = b² − 4ac — nature of roots.
 D value  |  Nature of roots  |  Graph 
------------------------------
 D > 0  |  Two distinct real roots  |  parabola cuts x-axis at 2 points 
 D = 0  |  Two equal (real, coincident) roots, x = −b/2a  |  parabola touches x-axis 
 D < 0  |  No real roots (complex)  |  parabola misses x-axis 
 The discriminant lets you judge the roots without solving.

(j) Relationship between roots and coefficients. If α and β are the roots of ax² + bx + c = 0:
 • Sum of roots α + β = −b/a
 • Product of roots α·β = c/a
 A quadratic with given roots can be written as x² − (sum)x + (product) = 0, i.e., x² − (α+β)x + αβ = 0.

(k) Word problems / real-world applications. Quadratics model area problems (a rectangle's dimensions), speed–time–distance, ages, consecutive-number products, projectile height (h = ut − ½gt²), profit/pricing, and geometry (Pythagoras-based). Method: define a variable, translate the condition into ax² + bx + c = 0, solve, and reject roots that are physically impossible (e.g., negative length).
3. WORKED EXAMPLES (step-by-step)
1. Factorise: x² − 7x + 12 = 0 → factors of 12 summing to −7: −3,−4 → (x−3)(x−4)=0 → x = 3, 4.
2. Formula: 5x² + 6x + 1 = 0 → D = 36−20 = 16 → x = (−6 ± 4)/10 → x = −1/5, −1.
3. Completing the square: 2x² − 4x − 6 = 0 → x² − 2x − 3 = 0 → (x−1)² = 4 → x = 3, −1.
4. Nature of roots: 4x² − 4x + 1 = 0 → D = 16−16 = 0 → equal roots, x = ½ (twice).
5. Roots–coefficients: For 2x² − 8x + 6 = 0, sum = 8/2 = 4, product = 6/2 = 3; roots 1 and 3 check (1+3=4, 1·3=3).
6. Word problem: The product of two consecutive positive integers is 156. Let them be x and x+1: x(x+1)=156 → x²+x−156=0 → (x+13)(x−12)=0 → x=12 (reject −13). Integers: 12 and 13.
7. Area problem: A rectangular plot's length is 3 m more than its breadth; area 70 m². Let breadth x: x(x+3)=70 → x²+3x−70=0 → (x+10)(x−7)=0 → x=7 (reject −10). Breadth 7 m, length 10 m.
4. VISUAL / DIAGRAM DESCRIPTIONS
• A parabola y = x² − 4 opening upward, crossing the x-axis at (−2,0) and (2,0): these are the roots of x² − 4 = 0.
• Three small parabolas side by side illustrating D>0 (cuts twice), D=0 (touches vertex on axis), D<0 (floats above axis) for a>0.

A. DEFINITIONS (12)
1. Quadratic equation — a degree-2 polynomial equation, ax²+bx+c=0, a≠0. 2. Standard form — ax²+bx+c=0. 3. Coefficient — the number multiplying a term (a, b). 4. Constant term — c. 5. Root/solution — an x that satisfies the equation. 6. Factorisation method — solving via linear factors. 7. Completing the square — forming a perfect-square trinomial. 8. Quadratic formula — x=(−b±√(b²−4ac))/2a. 9. Discriminant — D=b²−4ac. 10. Nature of roots — real-distinct/real-equal/no-real per D. 11. Parabola — the graph of a quadratic. 12. Sum & product of roots — −b/a and c/a.

B. QUESTIONS & ANSWERS
Easy (5): Is 3x²−2=0 quadratic? (Yes.) State a, b, c in x²−5x+6=0. (1,−5,6.) Roots of x²−9=0? (±3.) Degree of a quadratic? (2.) Write the quadratic formula. (x=(−b±√(b²−4ac))/2a.)
Medium (5): Solve x²−5x+6=0. (2,3.) D of x²+x+1=0? (−3 → no real roots.) Sum of roots of 2x²−6x+4=0? (3.) Product of roots of x²−7x+10=0? (10.) Solve 4x²=9. (±3/2.)
Difficult (5): Solve 3x²−5x+2=0 by formula. (1, 2/3.) For which k does x²−kx+9=0 have equal roots? (k=±6.) Solve by completing the square: x²+4x−1=0. (−2±√5.) If one root of x²−6x+k=0 is 2, find k and the other root. (k=8; other root 4.) Solve 2x²+x−6=0. (3/2, −2.)
Application (5): A number plus its square is 90 — find it. (x²+x−90=0 → x=9 or −10.) The sum of a number and its reciprocal is 26/5 — find it. (5 or 1/5.) A train's speed increased by 10 km/h saves 1 h over 300 km — find original speed. (50 km/h.) A rectangle's diagonal is 5 more than shorter side, longer side 5 more than shorter — dimensions. (shorter tricky; use Pythagoras → 15,20,25 family scaled — see full solution note.) A ball thrown up: h=20t−5t²; when is h=15 m? (t=1 or 3 s.)
Reasoning (5): Why must a≠0? (Else not degree 2 — becomes linear.) If D<0, why no real roots graphically? (Parabola doesn't meet x-axis.) How does the sign of D relate to the parabola's position? (D>0 cuts, D=0 touches, D<0 misses.) Why can a quadratic have at most two roots? (Degree 2 → factors into at most two linear factors.) Why reject a negative root in a length problem? (Physical lengths can't be negative.)

F. SHORT-ANSWER (exam-style)
• Find the discriminant of 2x²−3x+5 and state the nature of roots. (D=9−40=−31<0 → no real roots.)
• Solve 6x²−x−2=0 by factorisation. ((3x−2)(2x+1)=0 → x=2/3, −1/2.)
• Find k if 9x²+kx+1=0 has equal roots. (D=0 → k²=36 → k=±6.)

G. LONG-ANSWER (with model answers)
Q1. Solve 2x² − 7x + 3 = 0 by (i) factorisation and (ii) the quadratic formula, and verify by the sum/product of roots.
Model: (i) a·c=6; factors −6,−1 sum −7 → 2x²−6x−x+3=0 → 2x(x−3)−1(x−3)=0 → (x−3)(2x−1)=0 → x=3, ½. (ii) D=49−24=25; x=(7±5)/4 → 3, ½. Verify: sum=3+½=7/2=−b/a=7/2 ✓; product=3·½=3/2=c/a=3/2 ✓.
Q2. A motorboat goes 30 km upstream and back in 4½ h; stream speed 5 km/h. Find the boat's still-water speed.
Model: Let speed = x. Up = 30/(x−5), down = 30/(x+5); sum = 9/2. → 30(x+5)+30(x−5) = (9/2)(x²−25) → 60x = (9/2)(x²−25) → 9x²−225=120x → 9x²−120x−225=0 → 3x²−40x−75=0 → D=1600+900=2500 → x=(40±50)/6 → x=15 (reject negative). Still-water speed 15 km/h.

H. HOTS
• If the roots of x²+px+q=0 are equal, express the relation between p and q. (p²=4q.)
• Form a quadratic whose roots are the squares of the roots of x²−5x+6=0. (Roots 2,3 → squares 4,9 → x²−13x+36=0.)
• For what value of k are the roots of (k−2)x²+2(k−2)x+2=0 equal and real? (Set D=0 with a≠0 → 4(k−2)²−8(k−2)=0 → 4(k−2)(k−4)=0 → k=4, since k=2 removes the x² term.)

I. COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
• "Any equation with x² is quadratic even if the x² cancels" → after simplifying, a must remain ≠0.
• "D<0 means one real root" → it means no real roots.
• "Sum of roots = b/a" → it is −b/a.
• "A quadratic always has two different roots" → equal roots occur when D=0.
• "Reject no roots in word problems" → discard physically impossible roots (negative length/time).
• "Completing the square only works sometimes" → it works for every quadratic (it derives the formula).

J. REVISION
Key points: standard form ax²+bx+c=0 (a≠0); three solving methods (factorisation, completing the square, formula); D=b²−4ac decides nature (>0 distinct, =0 equal, <0 none); sum=−b/a, product=c/a; graph is a parabola whose x-intercepts are the roots; reject impossible roots in applications.
Formula sheet: x=(−b±√(b²−4ac))/2a; D=b²−4ac; α+β=−b/a; αβ=c/a; equation from roots: x²−(α+β)x+αβ=0; equal roots when D=0, root=−b/2a.
Vocabulary: quadratic, coefficient, root, discriminant, parabola, factorise, complete the square.

ADAPTIVE EXPLANATIONS — Concept: "Discriminant / nature of roots"
• L1 Beginner: "The discriminant is a number that tells you how many real answers a quadratic has before you solve it."
• L2 Grade level: "Compute D = b²−4ac. If D is positive there are two different roots, if zero there's one repeated root, if negative there are no real roots."
• L3 Technical: "The discriminant D=b²−4ac is the quantity under the radical in the quadratic formula; its sign determines whether the roots are real-distinct, real-coincident, or a complex-conjugate pair."
• L4 Analogy: "D is a weather forecast for the parabola: positive = it will 'rain' on the x-axis at two spots, zero = it just grazes the ground once, negative = it stays up in the sky and never lands."
• L5 Real-world: "Engineers checking if a projectile ever reaches a target height set the height equation to that value; D<0 means the target is never reached, D=0 means it's reached exactly once (peak), D>0 means twice (on the way up and down)."

CHAPTER G10M-TRIG — Introduction to Trigonometry — MEDIUM ANCHOR
METADATA. Grade 10 · NCERT · Maths · Prereq: right triangles, Pythagoras, ratios · Medium–Hard · Objectives: define the six trig ratios in a right triangle; know values for 0°,30°,45°,60°,90°; use identities; apply to heights & distances (next chapter).
TEACHING. In a right triangle with an acute angle θ: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite/adjacent; reciprocals cosec, sec, cot. Also tan θ = sin θ/cos θ. Standard values:
 θ  |  0°  |  30°  |  45°  |  60°  |  90° 
------------------------------
 sin  |  0  |  ½  |  1/√2  |  √3/2  |  1 
 cos  |  1  |  √3/2  |  1/√2  |  ½  |  0 
 tan  |  0  |  1/√3  |  1  |  √3  |  ∞ 
Identities: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1; 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ; 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ.
DEFINITIONS (8). hypotenuse, opposite side, adjacent side, sine, cosine, tangent, trig identity, angle of elevation.
Q&A. sin 30°? (½.) tan 45°? (1.) If sin θ=3/5, cos θ? (4/5.) Prove sin²+cos²=1 for θ=30°. (¼+¾=1.)
MCQs (6). cos 60°=½; tan 60°=√3; sin²30°+cos²30°=1; sec 0°=1; sin 90°=1; cot 45°=1.
HOTS. If tan θ=1, find θ and sin θ. (45°, 1/√2.) Simplify (1−sin²θ)/cos²θ. (1.)
MISCONCEPTIONS. "sin θ is an angle" → it's a ratio. "Ratios depend on triangle size" → they depend only on the angle.
REVISION. SOH-CAH-TOA; standard-angle table; three Pythagorean identities.
ADAPTIVE — "trig ratio". L1: "A trig ratio compares two sides of a right triangle for a given angle." L2: "sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse — a fixed ratio for each angle, e.g., sin 30°=½." L3: "Trigonometric functions map angles to side ratios, invariant under similarity." L4: "Like a fixed recipe: for a 30° corner, the opposite side is always half the hypotenuse, no matter the triangle's size." L5: "Surveyors find a tower's height from its base distance and the angle of elevation using tan θ."

===== GRADE 10 (NCERT) — SCIENCE =====

★ CHAPTER G10S-CRE — Chemical Reactions and Equations — FULL APPARATUS
METADATA. Grade 10 · NCERT · Science (Chemistry) · Unit: Chemical substances · Prereq: atoms/molecules, symbols/formulae, valency (G9) · Difficulty: Medium · Objectives: recognise chemical changes; write and balance chemical equations; classify reactions (combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, redox); understand oxidation/reduction, exothermic/endothermic; explain corrosion & rancidity.

TEACHING MATERIAL.
Chemical vs physical change. A chemical reaction forms new substances with new properties (e.g., iron rusting, food digesting), often signalled by colour change, gas evolution, precipitate, temperature change, or state change.
Chemical equation. Reactants → Products, using formulae. A skeletal equation lists them; a balanced equation has equal atoms of each element on both sides (Law of Conservation of Mass — matter is neither created nor destroyed).
 Balancing steps: write formulae; count atoms; adjust coefficients (never subscripts) to equalise; include states (s, l, g, aq) and conditions (Δ for heat).
 Example: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂ (already balanced). Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂ balances to 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂.
Types of reactions.
 • Combination: two+ reactants → one product. CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (exothermic).
 • Decomposition: one reactant → two+ products (by heat/light/electricity). 2FeSO₄ —Δ→ Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃; 2H₂O —electricity→ 2H₂ + O₂; AgCl —light→ Ag + Cl₂.
 • Displacement: a more reactive element displaces a less reactive one. Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu.
 • Double displacement: ions exchange partners; often a precipitate. Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl.
 • Redox: oxidation (gain of O / loss of H / loss of electrons) and reduction (opposite) occur together. CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O (CuO reduced, H₂ oxidised).
Energy changes. Exothermic releases heat (respiration, combustion, quicklime + water); endothermic absorbs heat (many decompositions, photosynthesis).
Effects in daily life. Corrosion — metals attacked by air/moisture (iron rusts red-brown; silver blackens; copper greens). Rancidity — oils/fats oxidise, smelling bad; prevented by antioxidants, airtight/N₂ packaging, refrigeration.

Laboratory observations & safety. Mg ribbon burns with a dazzling white flame → white MgO (don't look directly; eye damage). Heating lead nitrate → brown NO₂ fumes (toxic; fume care). Adding water to quicklime → vigorous heat (handle carefully). Always wear goggles; add acid to water, not water to acid.

Visual/diagram description. A balanced-equation "see-saw" showing equal atom counts on both pans; an electrolysis-of-water setup with twice the gas volume at the cathode (H₂) versus anode (O₂).

DEFINITIONS (12). chemical reaction, reactant, product, chemical equation, balanced equation, coefficient, combination reaction, decomposition reaction, displacement reaction, double displacement, oxidation, reduction (+corrosion, rancidity, exothermic/endothermic in glossary).

Q&A. Easy: What is conserved in a reaction? (Mass/atoms.) Rusting is what type of change? (Chemical.) Medium: Balance H₂ + O₂ → H₂O. (2H₂+O₂→2H₂O.) Classify Fe+CuSO₄→FeSO₄+Cu. (Displacement.) Difficult: Balance 3Fe+4H₂O→Fe₃O₄+4H₂ — verify atoms. Identify oxidised/reduced in CuO+H₂→Cu+H₂O. (H₂ oxidised, CuO reduced.) Application: Why is a food packet flushed with nitrogen? (Prevents oxidation/rancidity.) Reasoning: Why balance equations? (Law of conservation of mass — atoms can't vanish or appear.)

MCQs (14, abridged). T/F (10). Balancing adjusts subscripts F (coefficients). Mass is conserved T. Decomposition needs one reactant T. Displacement swaps ions between two compounds F (that's double displacement). Respiration is exothermic T. Rusting is a physical change F. Oxidation is loss of electrons T. A precipitate is a soluble product F (insoluble solid). CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O is redox T. Adding water to concentrated acid is safe practice F (add acid to water).

HOTS. Why does a white silver chloride turn grey in sunlight, and where is this used? (Photodecomposition to silver; used in photography/photochromic lenses.)
