★ FLAGSHIP G12AC-PART — Partnership: Fundamentals & Profit & Loss Appropriation — FULL APPARATUS
1. METADATA
• Grade 12 · NCERT/CBSE · Commerce · Accountancy · Unit: Partnership · Chapter: Accounting for Partnership — Basic Concepts
• Prerequisites: Class 11 journal/ledger/final accounts; the accounting equation; capital accounts.
• Difficulty: Medium–Hard (foundation for all partnership chapters and a major board-marks block).
• Objectives: understand partnership features & the deed; distinguish fixed vs fluctuating capital accounts; compute interest on capital/drawings, partner's salary/commission; prepare the Profit & Loss Appropriation Account and partners' capital/current accounts; handle guarantee of profit and past adjustments.
2. TEACHING MATERIAL
(a) Meaning & features. A partnership is a relation between persons who agree to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all (Indian Partnership Act, 1932). Features: two or more persons, an agreement, lawful business, profit sharing, mutual agency, unlimited liability.
(b) Partnership deed. The written agreement stating capital, profit-sharing ratio, interest on capital/drawings, salaries, etc. In the absence of a deed, the Act applies: no interest on capital, no salary, no interest on drawings; profits shared equally; interest on partner's loan @ 6% p.a.
(c) Capital accounts — two methods.
 • Fluctuating capital: all adjustments (interest, salary, drawings, share of profit) pass through the single Capital Account; balance changes each year.
 • Fixed capital: Capital Account stays fixed (only fresh capital/withdrawal of capital); all other items go to a separate Current Account.
(d) Appropriation of profit. Net profit (from the P&L A/c) is distributed among partners through the Profit & Loss Appropriation Account (an extension of P&L). It is debited with interest on capital, salaries/commission to partners and the share of profit transferred to partners; it is credited with net profit b/d and interest on drawings.
 Order: charge interest on capital and salaries first; the remaining profit is shared in the profit-sharing ratio.
(e) Interest on capital & drawings.
 • Interest on capital = Capital × rate × time (an appropriation, allowed only if the deed permits, and only out of profits unless agreed otherwise).
 • Interest on drawings = Drawings × rate × time (charged to the partner). For even monthly drawings, use the average period: for equal amounts drawn at month-end for 12 months, average period = 5.5 months; beginning of month = 6.5; mid-month = 6.
(f) Partner's salary/commission. Only if the deed allows. Commission may be "on net profit before charging such commission" (rate/100 × profit) or "after charging it" (rate/(100+rate) × profit).
(g) Guarantee of profit. A partner may be guaranteed a minimum; any deficiency is borne by the guaranteeing partner(s) in the agreed ratio.
3. WORKED EXAMPLES (step-by-step)
Example 1 — P&L Appropriation. A and B are partners sharing profits 3:2. Capitals: A ₹2,00,000, B ₹1,00,000. Deed: interest on capital 10% p.a.; B's salary ₹2,000/month; net profit for the year ₹90,000.
Step 1 — Interest on capital: A = 2,00,000×10% = ₹20,000; B = 1,00,000×10% = ₹10,000 (total ₹30,000).
Step 2 — B's salary = 2,000×12 = ₹24,000.
Step 3 — Profit left for sharing = 90,000 − 30,000 − 24,000 = ₹36,000. Share: A = 36,000×3/5 = ₹21,600; B = 36,000×2/5 = ₹14,400.
P&L Appropriation A/c:
 Dr  |  ₹  |  Cr  |  ₹ 
------------------------------
 Interest on Capital: A 20,000; B 10,000  |  30,000  |  Net Profit b/d  |  90,000 
 Salary to B  |  24,000  |    |  Profit transferred: A 21,600; B 14,400  |  36,000  |    |  Total  |  90,000  |  Total  |  90,000 
Example 2 — Interest on drawings. C withdrew ₹5,000 at the end of every month; interest @ 6% p.a.
Total drawings = 5,000×12 = ₹60,000. Average period (end of month) = 5.5 months. Interest = 60,000 × 6% × 5.5/12 = ₹1,650. (Or: 5,000×6%×(11+10+…+0)/12 = 5,000×6%×66/12 = 1,650.)

Example 3 — No deed. X and Y start with capitals ₹3,00,000 and ₹1,00,000; no deed; profit ₹40,000. Since no deed: no interest on capital/salary; profit shared equally → X ₹20,000, Y ₹20,000 (capital ratio is irrelevant without a deed).

Example 4 — Guarantee. P, Q, R share 3:2:1; R is guaranteed minimum ₹30,000; profit ₹1,20,000. Normal shares: P 60,000, Q 40,000, R 20,000. R short by ₹10,000; deficiency borne by P & Q in 3:2 → P bears 6,000, Q bears 4,000. Final: P 54,000, Q 36,000, R 30,000.
4. VISUAL / DIAGRAM DESCRIPTION
A flow: Net Profit (P&L A/c) → P&L Appropriation A/c (Dr: interest on capital, salaries, profit share; Cr: net profit, interest on drawings) → Partners' Capital/Current Accounts.

G. LONG-ANSWER (model, full solution)
Q: L and M are partners (3:2). Capitals ₹4,00,000 and ₹2,00,000. Deed: interest on capital 6% p.a.; M's salary ₹3,000/month; interest on drawings — L ₹1,200, M ₹800. Net profit before appropriations ₹1,00,000. Prepare the P&L Appropriation A/c and show profit shares.
Model: Interest on capital: L 24,000, M 12,000 (total 36,000). M's salary 36,000. Interest on drawings (credited): L 1,200 + M 800 = 2,000. Divisible profit = 1,00,000 + 2,000 − 36,000 − 36,000 = ₹30,000. Shares: L = 30,000×3/5 = 18,000; M = 30,000×2/5 = 12,000.
P&L Appropriation A/c: Dr — Interest on capital 36,000; Salary 36,000; Profit transferred (L 18,000, M 12,000) 30,000 = 1,02,000. Cr — Net profit 1,00,000; Interest on drawings 2,000 = 1,02,000. Totals ₹1,02,000 = ₹1,02,000 ✓.

H. HOTS
• If a firm's profit is insufficient to allow full interest on capital, how is interest allowed? (Proportionately, treating it as an appropriation — limited to available profit unless the deed makes it a charge.)
• Why can commission "after charging such commission" differ from "before"? (Different bases: rate/(100+rate) vs rate/100 of profit.)
• A partner draws unevenly through the year — which method computes interest on drawings? (Product method: sum of (amount × months) × rate/12.)

I. MISCONCEPTIONS
• "Profit is always shared in the capital ratio" → only if the deed says so; otherwise equally.
• "Interest on capital is always allowed" → only if the deed permits and (usually) out of profits.
• "Salary to a partner is an expense (charge)" → it's an appropriation of profit unless the deed makes it a charge.
• "Interest on drawings reduces the appropriation account's debit" → it's credited (increases divisible profit).

ADAPTIVE — Concept: "P&L Appropriation Account"
• L1 Beginner: "It's the account that shares out a partnership's profit among the partners."
• L2 Grade level: "After the P&L Account finds net profit, the Appropriation Account distributes it — first paying interest on capital and any partner salaries, then splitting what's left in the profit-sharing ratio."
• L3 Technical: "The P&L Appropriation Account is an extension of the P&L Account that allocates net profit to partners via interest on capital, remuneration and residual profit, while crediting interest on drawings, per the partnership deed."
• L4 Analogy: "Think of net profit as a cake: the Appropriation Account first cuts fixed slices (interest on capital, salaries) that the deed promises, then divides the remaining cake in each partner's agreed share."
• L5 Real-world: "Any real partnership firm — a law firm, a shop run by two friends — uses this to fairly settle who gets what at year-end, honouring who invested more or worked more."

★ CHAPTER G12AC-RATIO — Accounting Ratios — MEDIUM ANCHOR (numerical)
METADATA. Grade 12 · NCERT · Accountancy · Unit: Analysis of financial statements · Prereq: financial statements · Difficulty: Medium · Objectives: compute and interpret liquidity, solvency, activity and profitability ratios.
TEACHING. Ratio analysis expresses relationships between financial figures to judge performance.
 • Liquidity: Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities (ideal ≈ 2:1); Quick Ratio = (CA − Stock − Prepaid)/CL (ideal ≈ 1:1).
 • Solvency: Debt–Equity = Debt/Equity; Total Assets to Debt; Interest Coverage.
 • Activity/Turnover: Inventory Turnover = COGS/Avg Stock; Debtors Turnover = Net Credit Sales/Avg Debtors; Working Capital Turnover.
 • Profitability: Gross Profit Ratio = (GP/Sales)×100; Net Profit Ratio; Operating Ratio; Return on Investment.
Worked (step-by-step): Current Assets ₹4,00,000 (incl. stock ₹1,00,000), Current Liabilities ₹2,00,000. Current Ratio = 4,00,000/2,00,000 = 2:1. Quick Ratio = (4,00,000−1,00,000)/2,00,000 = 1.5:1.
DEFINITIONS (6). ratio analysis, current ratio, quick ratio, debt-equity ratio, inventory turnover, gross profit ratio.
Q&A/MCQ (condensed). Ideal current ratio: 2:1; quick ratio excludes stock & prepaid; GP ratio = (GP/Sales)×100; high debt-equity signals more risk; inventory turnover uses COGS/avg stock.
HOTS. A firm has a high current ratio but low quick ratio — what does that imply? (Large stock holdings; possible liquidity concern.)
ADAPTIVE — "current ratio". L1: "It checks if a business can pay its short-term bills." L2: "Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities; 2:1 means ₹2 of short-term assets for every ₹1 owed soon." L3: "A liquidity metric measuring short-term solvency; ratios well below/above the norm signal illiquidity/idle assets." L4: "Like checking if your wallet plus this month's income covers this month's bills — with a comfortable cushion." L5: "Lenders look at it before giving a firm a short-term loan, to be sure it can repay."

★ CHAPTER G12EC-NI — National Income & its Measurement (Macroeconomics) — MEDIUM ANCHOR
METADATA. Grade 12 · NCERT · Economics · Unit: Introductory Macroeconomics · Prereq: basic micro, aggregates · Difficulty: Medium · Objectives: define macro aggregates (GDP, GNP, NNP, NI); the three methods of measurement; distinguish nominal vs real GDP.
TEACHING. Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole (output, income, employment, price level). National income = the total value of final goods and services produced in a year.
 • GDP (Gross Domestic Product) — value of final output produced within a country's borders. GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad (NFIA). NNP = GNP − depreciation. National Income (NNP at factor cost) = NNP at market price − net indirect taxes.
 • Three measurement methods (all give the same total): Value-added (product) method (sum of value added, avoiding double counting), Income method (wages + rent + interest + profit = factor incomes), Expenditure method (C + I + G + (X − M)).
 • Real vs Nominal GDP: nominal uses current prices; real GDP uses base-year prices (removes inflation). GDP deflator = (Nominal/Real)×100.
Worked (step-by-step): Given C=500, I=200, G=150, X=80, M=50 → GDP (expenditure) = 500+200+150+(80−50) = ₹880. If NFIA = +20, GNP = 900; if depreciation = 40, NNP = 860; if net indirect taxes = 30, NI (NNP at FC) = ₹830.
DEFINITIONS (8). macroeconomics, GDP, GNP, NNP, national income, value added, real GDP, GDP deflator.
Q&A/MCQ (condensed). GDP counts final goods; GNP = GDP + NFIA; expenditure method: C+I+G+(X−M); real GDP uses base-year prices; NNP = GNP − depreciation; three methods give the same total.
HOTS. Why avoid double counting in the product method? (Intermediate goods' value is already in final goods.) Can nominal GDP rise while real GDP falls? (Yes — if prices rise faster than output.)
ADAPTIVE — "GDP". L1: "GDP is the total value of everything a country produces in a year." L2: "GDP adds up all FINAL goods and services made within a country's borders in a year — a measure of its economic size." L3: "GDP is the market value of final output within domestic territory over a period, computable by product, income or expenditure approaches which coincide by the circular-flow identity." L4: "GDP is like a country's annual 'total sales receipt' — how much new value everyone together created." L5: "News says 'GDP grew 7%' — that's real output rising, signalling more jobs, income and goods for people."

METADATA. Grade 12 · NCERT · Business Studies · Unit: Principles & functions of management · Prereq: forms of business · Difficulty: Medium · Objectives: define management; its nature & importance; Taylor's scientific & Fayol's principles; the functions of management.
TEACHING. Management is the process of getting things done through others to achieve goals effectively (right tasks) and efficiently (least resources). Nature: goal-oriented, all-pervasive, multidimensional, continuous, a group activity; both a science and an art, and an evolving profession.
